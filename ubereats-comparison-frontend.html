✓ Kafka + CQRS wins when...

You need low HTTP latency, high throughput, or resilience to downstream failures. Each service fails independently. Orders keep flowing even if payment-service is slow. Read/write DBs scale separately. Perfect for scale-out.

⚠ Blocking is fine when...

Your app is small, team is small, or you need strong consistency (e.g. inventory check must be synchronous). Simpler to debug and trace. No Kafka infra needed. Don't over-engineer if you have <1k orders/day.

CQRS read vs write

The Kafka approach separates the write DB (normalized, for commands) from the read DB (denormalized, for queries). Reads hit a flat pre-joined table — no locks, no joins. Kafka consumers project events into the read model asynchronously.

The tradeoff

Kafka + CQRS = eventual consistency. The read model may be milliseconds behind. The blocking approach = strong consistency — but you pay for it with latency and coupling. Choose based on your domain needs.
